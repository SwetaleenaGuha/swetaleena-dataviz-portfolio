## Target audience

The target audience for this story is people who are interested in travel and tourism in India but may not be familiar with how domestic tourism is distributed across different states and union territories.

# User research 
For user research, I spoke with three individuals who have some familiarity with India and/or an interest in travel. They did not need to have expertise in tourism or data visualization because I wanted to understand whether the story was clear to a general reader. I showed each participant the current draft of the story and visualizations and asked them to interpret the story without giving them detailed explanations beforehand.

### Interview introduction

Before showing participants the draft, I used the following short introduction:

I am developing a visual data story about how domestic tourism across India changed between 2020 and 2023. I would like you to look through the current draft as a general reader. I am testing the story and visualizations rather than you, so there are no right or wrong answers. As you look through it, please tell me anything that seems particularly clear, interesting, confusing, or difficult to interpret.

### Participants

I interviewed three graduate students in their 20s. All three were familiar with India and/or interested in travel, but none were participating as tourism or data visualization experts. This allowed me to test whether the story could be understood by general readers rather than specialists.

## Interview script

The goal of the interviews is to understand whether readers can follow the narrative, correctly interpret the visualizations, and identify the main takeaways without additional explanation. I also want to identify anything that feels confusing, unnecessary, or missing before developing the final version.

| Goal | Questions to Ask |
|------|------------------|
| Test the overall story | After looking through the draft, what do you think the main story is about? |
| Test visualization clarity | Which visualization was easiest to understand? Which was the most difficult, and why? |
| Test the narrative flow | Did the order of the visualizations feel logical to you? |
| Test key takeaways | What was the most important or interesting thing you learned from the story? |
| Identify confusion | Was there anything in the charts, labels, colors, or text that you found confusing? |
| Identify improvements | What is one thing you would change or add to make the story easier or more engaging to understand? |

The interviews were conversational, and feedback was captured as notes rather than verbatim transcripts; therefore, the findings below are paraphrased rather than presented as direct quotations.

## Interview findings

I conducted three informal interviews with friends who reviewed the draft visualizations and discussed the planned structure of the story with me. Overall, the feedback was positive, particularly regarding the clarity of the bar charts. However, the interviews also highlighted areas where the map and overall narrative flow could be improved.

| Area discussed | Interview 1 | Interview 2 | Interview 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall visuals | Responded positively to the visualizations but noticed that the India map was not displaying the colors clearly in the initial version. | Found the visualizations clear overall. | Responded positively to the visual approach and the direction of the story. |
| Easiest visualization to understand | Found the bar charts easy to interpret. | Found the bar charts easier to understand than the other visualizations. | Also found the bar charts straightforward and easy to read. |
| India map | Pointed out that the initial color treatment on the map did not communicate the differences between states clearly enough. | The map was understandable after the color treatment was improved. | Agreed that the geographic view was useful for the story. |
| Story structure | Suggested organizing the visualizations in a clearer sequence. | Felt that the order of the visuals could be improved to make the story easier to follow. | Also supported restructuring the story so that the visuals build on one another more naturally. |
| Personal travel photographs | Responded positively to my idea of incorporating photographs from places I have personally travelled to in India. | Felt that personal photographs could make the story more engaging. | Supported using my own travel experiences and photographs to add a more personal dimension to the data story. |

### Key takeaways

The most consistent feedback across the three interviews was that the bar charts were the easiest visualizations to understand. Another important observation concerned the India map. In the initial version, the color differences were not displayed clearly enough, so I revised the color treatment to make differences between states easier to see.

The participants also felt that the order of the visualizations could be improved. This made me reconsider the narrative sequence rather than treating each visualization as an independent chart.

Finally, I discussed the possibility of incorporating photographs from my own travels across India. The participants responded positively to this idea because it could connect the quantitative tourism data with actual destinations and make the final story feel more personal and engaging.

# Identified changes for Part III

Based on the user research, I identified several changes that I plan to make while developing the final version of the story.

| Research synthesis | Anticipated changes for Part III |
|---|---|
| The bar charts were consistently the easiest visualizations to understand. | Keep the bar charts as important elements of the final story and make sure their labels, titles, and annotations remain simple and readable. |
| The initial India map did not communicate differences between states clearly enough. | Improve the map's color scale and contrast so that differences in domestic tourist visits are more visible. |
| Participants suggested that the visualization order could be clearer. | Reorganize the narrative so that it progresses from the national trend, to leading destinations, to geographic distribution, and finally to changes between 2020 and 2023. |
| The current Shorthand draft is not yet fully organized. | Refine the Shorthand layout and create clearer transitions between the visualizations so that the story reads as one narrative rather than a collection of separate charts. |
| Participants responded positively to the idea of including my own travel photographs. | Experiment with incorporating selected photographs from places I have personally visited in India where they support the story, while keeping the data visualizations as the main focus. |

For Part III, my main priority will be improving the narrative flow and presentation of the story. The user research reinforced that the individual visualizations are generally understandable, but the way they are connected and presented will be important in making the final story engaging and easy to follow.
