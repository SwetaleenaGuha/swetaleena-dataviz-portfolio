# The final data story

Where India Travels: The Changing Geography of Domestic Tourism, 2020–2023

**[View my final data story on Shorthand](https://carnegiemellon.shorthandstories.com/where-india-travels/index.html)**

This project explores how domestic tourism in India changed between 2020 and 2023, focusing on the overall recovery in tourist visits, the states attracting the highest numbers of visitors, and differences in tourism growth across destinations.

I developed the story using Tableau for data visualization and Shorthand for digital storytelling. Alongside the data, I incorporated photographs from my own travels across India to create a more personal connection with the destinations represented in the analysis.

The final story brings together national trends, state-level comparisons, geographic patterns, and my personal interest in travelling across India.

# Changes made since Part II

Since completing Part II, I have focused on turning my initial collection of Tableau visualizations into a more cohesive data story in Shorthand. While the earlier version established the key findings, I wanted the final version to feel less like a series of individual charts and more like a narrative that guides readers through how domestic tourism in India has changed.

One of the most important changes came from my user interviews. All three participants found the bar charts relatively easy to understand, but one pointed out that the initial India map did not communicate differences between states clearly enough. Based on this feedback, I revised the map's color treatment to improve the visibility of geographic patterns. I also paid more attention to how the visualizations were presented, adding explanatory text and highlighting findings such as Uttar Pradesh's position as the leading destination in 2023 and the differences in growth across states.

Another major improvement was the organization of the story. I structured the final narrative to begin with the overall growth in domestic tourism, followed by the leading destinations, geographic distribution, and comparisons between 2020 and 2023. I also added a section discussing possible factors behind the increase in domestic tourism, drawing on external sources to provide context beyond the numbers.

The most personal change was incorporating photographs from my own travels across India. I have visited many destinations myself, and travel is something I genuinely enjoy. During the interviews, I discussed the idea of adding my photographs, and the response was positive. I decided to include them in the Shorthand story to connect the statistical patterns with the actual places and experiences that inspired my interest in this topic.

Overall, Part III has been about improving the clarity, flow, and presentation of the story while maintaining the original focus on understanding how domestic tourism across India changed between 2020 and 2023.

## The audience

The primary audience for my data story is people who are interested in travelling within India and understanding how tourism patterns differ across the country. I wanted the story to appeal to both people who have travelled extensively within India and those who are simply curious about the country's destinations. Since the audience may not have a background in data analysis, my goal was to communicate the findings through straightforward visualizations and an accessible narrative.

To better understand my audience, I conducted three informal interviews with graduate students who reviewed my draft visualizations and discussed the proposed story structure. Their feedback helped me identify which elements were already working and which needed improvement. All three found the bar charts relatively easy to interpret, while one participant pointed out that the initial color scheme of the India map made geographic differences difficult to distinguish. They also suggested improving the order of the visualizations so that readers could follow the story more naturally.

I incorporated this feedback by refining the map's colors, keeping the bar charts simple, and organizing the narrative from the national tourism trend to state-level rankings, geographic patterns, and changes between 2020 and 2023. I also added short explanations and key findings alongside the visualizations so that readers could understand their significance without needing to interpret every number independently.

Another consideration was making the story engaging beyond the data itself. When I shared my idea of including photographs from my own travels, the interviewees responded positively. I incorporated these photographs into the final Shorthand story to create a more personal connection with the destinations while keeping the data visualizations central to the narrative.

Overall, the audience feedback reinforced the importance of balancing analytical clarity with engaging storytelling. My aim was to create a story that readers could understand and enjoy, regardless of their familiarity with tourism statistics or data visualization.

## Final design decisions

One of my main design goals was to make the story understandable to readers who may not be familiar with tourism statistics. Rather than presenting all the data at once, I chose four visualizations that answer different questions and build on one another.

I started with a line chart showing how domestic tourist visits increased between 2020 and 2023. This establishes the overall trend before introducing more detailed comparisons. I then used a horizontal bar chart to rank the leading states in 2023, since bars make it easier to compare destinations directly. The India map adds a geographic perspective, showing how visits were distributed across different parts of the country. Finally, the comparison chart places 2020 and 2023 side by side to highlight how the recovery differed across leading destinations.

Color was another important consideration. After receiving feedback that the original India map was difficult to interpret, I adjusted its color treatment to make geographic differences more visible. I also used contrasting colors in the 2020 versus 2023 comparison chart to help readers distinguish between the two years.

For the Shorthand presentation, I experimented with different layouts, background images, and text sections. I used short, takeaway-oriented headings and explanatory captions to help readers understand the significance of each visualization. I also included a section discussing possible factors behind the growth in domestic tourism, while distinguishing the observed data trends from potential explanations.

A particularly meaningful decision was incorporating photographs from my own travels across India. Since travelling is one of my personal interests, I wanted the story to reflect that connection rather than feel like a purely statistical report. The photographs add a personal element, while the Tableau visualizations remain the primary source of evidence.

The biggest lesson I learned was that creating a compelling data story involves more than producing accurate charts. The order of the visuals, clarity of the labels, use of color, and surrounding narrative all influence how readers interpret the information. Working through Tableau, Shorthand, and user feedback helped me understand how these elements can work together to communicate a clearer story.

# Final thoughts

Working on this project has been one of the most enjoyable parts of my Telling Stories with Data course because it allowed me to combine data analysis with something I personally love: travelling across India.

At the beginning, I was primarily focused on identifying trends in the government tourism dataset and presenting them through Tableau. As the project progressed, I realized that finding interesting numbers was only one part of the challenge. The more difficult task was deciding which findings mattered most, how to organize them, and how to communicate them to someone seeing the data for the first time.

The user interviews were particularly valuable because they helped me see the visualizations from a reader's perspective. Feedback about the map colors and the order of the charts reminded me that a visualization can be technically correct without necessarily being easy to understand. Revisiting those decisions helped me improve the final story.

I especially enjoyed incorporating photographs from my own travels. They made the project feel more personal and gave me an opportunity to connect my experiences with the broader patterns in domestic tourism across India.

If I had more time, I would explore tourism trends at the district level and investigate how factors such as transportation access, infrastructure, and major tourist attractions might help explain differences between states. I would also be interested in comparing total visits with population-adjusted measures to provide another perspective on tourism activity.

Overall, this project helped me become more thoughtful about the relationship between data, visual design, and storytelling. My biggest takeaway is that a good data story should not simply show what the numbers say. It should help readers understand why those numbers are interesting and encourage them to explore the topic further.
